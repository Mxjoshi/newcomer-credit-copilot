The decision, three ways
Same engine. One of three honest outcomes.

Pick an outcome. The score and verdict are deterministic; the copilot only writes the words.

The problem

A newcomer starts with a blank credit file.

A new arrival starts with a blank AECB file, with no record at the UAE credit bureau. Banks reject good customers, or assess them slowly by hand.

The product

Rules decide. The model only explains.

The officer enters the applicant's details. A rules-based judgmental scorecard assesses repay-vs-default risk, a policy check runs UAE lending rules, and an LLM writes a plain-language explanation. The score and the verdict are fully deterministic: the LLM writes the explanation only, and cannot move a single point, rule, or recommendation.

The product calls I made
01
Locked the 24-case ground truth before building the scorecard. So the design could not be tuned to the answer key.
02
Conservative error stance. A bad loan costs more than a missed one, so v1 leans to refer and decline.
03
Rules plus an LLM, no trained model. For a regulated v1, explainability and defensibility beat a marginal accuracy gain.
04
Corrected my own positioning after research, instead of overclaiming what the tool does.
Result

On the 24-case synthetic set, first-pass weights from domain reasoning alone scored 20 of 24 with zero false approvals. Targets: 80%+ decision accuracy, under 10% false approvals, 100% of claims traceable to policy.
